Phase-out

The cost arrives in 2030

Importers only pay for emissions above the EU benchmark's free allocation. As that allocation falls, the charge on every tonne rises. Between 2029 and 2030 the exposed share more than doubles, from 22.5% to 48.5%, and the charge on a tonne of hot-rolled coil rises about 44%.

How it works

Two scores from public filings

  1. Read the filings

    Claude extracts Scope 1 and 2, production and energy from BRSR, annual reports and credit-rating notes. Each figure carries its source page.

  2. Price the carbon

    We apply the product's EU benchmark, the year's free allocation and three EU price paths, then convert to rupees against the firm's EBITDA.

  3. Score sustainability

    Emissions, transition plan, disclosure and efficiency roll into a 0–100 score. A tier of A, B or C says how much rests on verified data.

From what CBAM is to how a bank should read a readiness score. Each part links back to the course session it draws on.

1. What CBAM is 2. The formula 3. India's position 4. Course map 5. Glossary 6. Check yourself

1. What CBAM is

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism charges EU importers for the carbon emitted when certain goods are made abroad, so that imports pay the same carbon price as EU production under the EU ETS. It covers iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity.

What changed on 1 January 2026

The transitional reporting phase ended. Importers bringing in more than 50 tonnes a year of covered goods must be authorised declarants and buy certificates for 2026 imports. Certificate sales open on 1 February 2027, and the first annual declaration is due on 30 September 2027.

Who actually pays

The EU importer buys the certificates. In practice the cost flows back to the Indian exporter through a lower price or a lost order. CarbonSetu assumes the exporter absorbs it in full; you can change that on the calculator.

Direct vs indirect emissions

Direct emissions come from the plant itself. Indirect emissions come from the electricity it uses, including power from its own captive plant. For steel, aluminium and hydrogen, CBAM prices only direct emissions today. For cement and fertilisers it prices both, using India's default grid factor of 0.726 tCO₂/MWh.

Default values and why they hurt

An exporter that can't supply verified data is charged at the EU's default value for its country and product, plus a mark-up: 10% in 2026, 20% in 2027 and 30% from 2028 (1% for fertilisers). India's default for hot-rolled steel is 4.28 tCO₂e/t, against Tata Steel's reported 2.45.

2. The formula

Certificates owed on one tonne equal its embedded emissions minus the free allocation an EU plant would get for the same product.

CBAM cost = Volume × max(0, SEE − BM × FA) × (PEU − PIndia)
Worked example: 1 t of JSW hot-rolled coil in 2030

SEE = 2.36 tCO₂e/t (JSW's reported FY26 intensity). BM = 1.370 for the BF/BOF route. FA = 51.5% in 2030.

2.36 − 1.370 × 0.515 = 1.654 tCO₂e owed

1.654 × €126 = €208 per tonne ≈ ₹22,100

On the EU default the same tonne owes 4.28 × 1.3 − 0.706 = 4.86 t, or €612.

3. India's position

  • India exported €5.30 bn of CBAM goods to the EU in 2025, 83% of it iron and steel.
  • The EU–India FTA, concluded on 27 January 2026, gives no CBAM exemption. A CBAM annex sets a work plan on verifiers and crediting Indian carbon prices.
  • The Carbon Credit Trading Scheme set intensity targets for aluminium and cement in 2025. Steel targets were in draft in mid-2026. The EU does not yet recognise the CCTS for deductions.
  • The Green Steel Taxonomy (Dec 2024) calls steel green below 2.2 tCO₂e/t. No large Indian producer qualifies yet.

How the data pipeline works

  1. CollectBRSR, annual reports and credit-rating notes are pulled from NSE, BSE and rating-agency sites.
  2. ExtractClaude reads each filing and returns emissions, energy and output with the page and quote for every figure. Try it.
  3. CheckUnits are normalised; Scope 1 + 2 ÷ output is compared with the reported intensity; gaps over 10% are flagged for review.
  4. ScoreThe CBAM cost model and the Sustainability Index Score run on the checked figures; each gets a confidence tier.
  5. Refreshn8n fetches the quarterly CBAM certificate price and new filings, then recomputes. The production dashboard is built in Lovable or Replit.

Firm data and sources

Secondary sources such as cbamguide.com were used for benchmark values; check them against the Official Journal text of Implementing Regulations (EU) 2025/2620 and 2025/2621 before relying on them. Not investment advice.

CarbonSetu investor case

An annual-subscription ratings business for a market that turned real on 1 January 2026. The plan below is fully adjustable; every figure is a planning assumption.

Problem

Banks, EU buyers and investors can't tell which Indian exporters are exposed to CBAM or prepared for it. Unlisted firms have no rating at all.

Solution

A Sustainability Index Score and EBITDA-at-Risk in rupees for every firm in CBAM sectors, with a confidence tier on each score.

Why now

Certificates for 2026 imports go on sale on 1 Feb 2027. The free-allocation cliff hits in 2030. Verified data saves a steelmaker up to ₹31,000 crore by 2034.

Customers

Banks and NBFCs, EU importers, Indian producers, and government or think tanks, on annual subscriptions.

Edge

Claude reads filings in minutes, so unlisted firms can be scored cheaply. Free calculators exist; none rate readiness or serve lenders.

Ask
